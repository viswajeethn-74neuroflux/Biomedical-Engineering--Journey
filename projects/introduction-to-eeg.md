Introduction to EEG

1. Project Overview

This project explores electroencephalography (EEG), a technology used to record electrical activity from the brain. EEG is a non-invasive technique widely used in clinical medicine and neuroscience research to study brain activity.

2. Objective

Understand the basic principles of EEG.

Learn how EEG signals are detected and recorded.

Explore the clinical applications of EEG.

Understand the role of biomedical engineers in EEG instrumentation.

3. How EEG Works

The brain contains billions of neurons that communicate through electrical and chemical signals. The combined electrical activity of groups of neurons produces small voltage differences that can be measured at the scalp.

EEG electrodes placed on the scalp detect these voltage differences. The EEG system amplifies and processes the signals and displays them as waveforms over time.

The resulting waveforms can be analyzed to study patterns of brain activity. EEG recordings are interpreted alongside clinical information by qualified healthcare professionals.

4. Applications

EEG has several applications in medicine and neuroscience:

Epilepsy assessment: Helps healthcare professionals evaluate electrical activity associated with seizures.

Sleep studies: Helps identify brain activity patterns during different stages of sleep.

Brain research: Allows researchers to investigate brain activity during different tasks and cognitive processes.

Clinical monitoring: Can be used to monitor brain activity in certain critical care settings.

Neurological assessment: May help evaluate suspected abnormalities in brain function.

5. Role of Biomedical Engineers

Biomedical engineers contribute to the design, testing, maintenance, and improvement of EEG equipment.

Their responsibilities may include:

Understanding EEG instrumentation and signal acquisition systems.

Evaluating equipment performance and signal quality.

Inspecting electrodes, cables, connectors, and related components.

Supporting preventive maintenance and troubleshooting according to established procedures.

Helping ensure equipment safety and reliable operation.

Contributing to the development of improved biomedical signal-processing technologies.

6. What I Learned

Through this project, I learned that EEG is a non-invasive method of recording electrical activity from the brain. I understood how scalp electrodes detect small voltage differences and how EEG systems convert these signals into waveforms for analysis.

I also explored the use of EEG in epilepsy assessment, sleep studies, and neuroscience research. Finally, I learned how biomedical engineers contribute to the reliability, safety, and development of EEG instrumentation.
